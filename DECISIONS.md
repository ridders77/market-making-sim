### Calibration method
- 2026-11-26 — Touch estimator, ask side uses buyer-initiated prints only (is_buyer_maker = False) and bid side seller-initiated only. — Only an aggressive buy can fill a resting ask.
- 2026-11-26 — δ grid 0 … 8 USDT in 0.1 steps; δ is measured from the mid in force just before the window starts. Exponential fit over δ ∈ [0.1, 5.0] USDT; δ = 0 (a print at the best quote) is excluded from the fit and its rate reported separately. — λ(δ) jumps by ~15× between δ = 0 and δ = 0.1: on BTCUSDT someone at the touch is hit about twice per second, but a print one tick beyond the touch needs the price to move or a sweep. ln λ is close to linear from 0.1 to 8 USDT. Excluding the touch is equivalent to assuming we sit at the back of the queue at the best price, which is the honest assumption for a new market maker.
- 2026-11-26 — Main window Δt = 1 s (the smallest the 1-second mids allow); Δt = 2 s and 5 s are reported as a sensitivity check. Points with p = 1 or fewer than 30 hits are excluded from the fit (none are in range at Δt = 1 s).
- 2026-11-26 — σ main estimate = realised volatility of 1-second mid changes (zero-mean MLE); 10 s and 60 s versions are reported as a microstructure check. — Specified by the owner; the comparison shows whether 1 s sampling is biased.
- 2026-11-26 — Bootstrap CIs resample days (not windows). — Windows within a day share that day's volatility, so days are the independent units.
- 2026-11-26 — Pre-registered out-of-sample verdict: "good" if the calibration-day fit predicts test-day λ(δ) within a factor 1.5 on average over the fit range (mean |ln(obs/pred)| < 0.4), "usable with care" up to a factor 2 (< 0.7), otherwise "poor". — Criterion fixed before seeing test results.

### Stage 2 simulation results (results/stage2/strategy_comparison.md)
- A–S vs naive with the same average spread, 1,000 paired paths, no fee: means are statistically indistinguishable at γ = 1e-5 and 1e-4 in all regimes. At γ = 1e-3, A–S is lower by 29 / 82 / 10 USDT (low / medium / high). P&L std is 1.6–9× lower for A–S (e.g. medium, γ = 1e-4: 195 vs 1,251 USDT/h; std of final inventory 5.0 vs 19.2 BTC). This is the paper's pattern, at real scale.
- Fees decide profitability: A–S captures 1.65 / 2.06 / 2.57 USDT per fill (low / medium / high, ≈ 1/k whatever γ, because fills land mostly on the side skewed towards the mid), while the fee is ~2.7 USDT at 1 bp and ~5.5 at 2 bp (S ≈ 27,500). Every configuration loses money at 1 and 2 bp (e.g. medium, γ = 1e-4: −262 at 1 bp, −1,306 at 2 bp per hour). All are profitable at 0 bp and with the −0.5 bp rebate (+782 and +1,304).
- With fees, A–S at γ = 1e-3 does worse than naive because it trades more (219 vs 125 fills/h in medium). Its spread narrows towards T and its skew keeps one side close to the mid, while the naive quote stays at the wide average spread.
- Reality check: A–S's optimal spread is 33–165× the real 1-tick spread (3.3–16.5 USDT vs 0.1). The fill-intensity curve is flat (k ≈ 0.4–0.6 per USDT: moving one more USDT away loses only ~40% of fills), so the formula, which assumes we are the only market maker, prices like a monopolist. The real book is competitive: many makers quote at the touch, priority goes by queue position, and the top makers are paid rebates and are profitable at 1 tick because of the rebate and the 1.9/s flow at the touch, not the spread. A–S has no competitors, no queue and no fee term. A fee-aware optimum per side is roughly 1/k + fee·S ≈ 7.5 USDT at 2 bp, wider still.

### Step 1 pre-registration: choice of the fill-intensity model (written and committed before any test-day score is computed)
- 2026-11-26 — **Data.** λ(δ) uses the stage 3 lot rule: with a 0.01 BTC lot, a 1 s window counts as an ask fill at δ only if the buyer-initiated volume printed at prices ≥ mid₀ + δ within the window is ≥ 0.01 BTC (the bid side is the mirror image with seller-initiated volume at ≤ mid₀ − δ). mid₀ = the mid in force when the window opens. Δt = 1 s, ask and bid pooled. Only the 10 calibration days are used for fitting.
- **Candidate (a), relative units.** δ in bps of mid₀ (δ_bps = 10⁴·δ/mid₀), σ in bps. Touch curve on a 0.01 bp grid. Fixed A_rel (/s) and k_rel (/bp) fitted by OLS of ln λ on δ_bps over δ_bps ∈ [0.03, 1.80] (≈ the stage 2 range of 0.1–5 USDT at the calibration price of ~27.8k, starting beyond the touch at every price level), pooled over calibration days, dropping points with < 30 hits.
- **Candidate (b), σ-conditional.** Trailing σ̂ = EWMA (pandas `ewm(span=30)`, i.e. weight α = 2/31 per minute) of squared 60 s mid changes on the UTC-minute grid, divided by 60 s, using only changes that ended at or before the window opens. Windows in the first 30 minutes of a day have no σ̂ and are excluded (no fill-in). The calibration windows (all 10 days pooled) are split into 10 deciles of σ̂; in each decile A and k are fitted in USDT over δ ∈ [0.1, 5.0] (the stage 2 range); then A = a·σ̂^b and k = c·σ̂^d by log-log OLS on the decile medians of σ̂.
- **Score.** For each test day: evaluation windows = windows opening at or after 00:30 UTC with a valid mid₀ and σ̂ (the same windows for both models). For δ on the USDT tick grid {0.1, 0.2, …, 5.0}: p_obs(δ) = share of (window, side) pairs with a lot fill at δ; p_pred(δ) = the mean over the same windows of 1 − exp(−λ_model(δ)·Δt), where λ_model uses only information available when the window opens ((a): A_rel·exp(−k_rel·10⁴δ/mid₀); (b): a·σ̂^b·exp(−c·σ̂^d·δ)). Both are converted to intensities with λ = −ln(1 − p)/Δt. score_day = the mean over δ of |ln(λ_pred/λ_obs)|; δ points with < 30 observed hits on that day are dropped and counted. **Model score = the mean of score_day over the 10 test days** (95% bootstrap CI over days).
- **Rule (fixed now).** Pass mark: score ≤ 0.4 (about a factor of 1.5). Choose (a) if it passes; otherwise (b) if it passes; otherwise fall back to the paper parameters and say so. Both scores are reported whatever happens. For reference only (not part of the rule), the stage 2 pooled-USDT fixed A, k is scored the same way.
- **Use in the simulator.** If (a): regime σ_60s in bps and k_rel are converted to USDT at the regime's price level S₀ (σ = σ_bps·S₀/10⁴, k = k_rel·10⁴/S₀, A = A_rel). If (b): A and k are evaluated at the regime's σ_60s.
- **Step 1 result (2026-11-26, computed after the pre-registration commit c241c67).** Scores (mean over test days of mean |ln(λ_pred/λ_obs)|, 95% CI over days): **(a) relative units 0.504 [0.272, 0.761], fails; (b) σ-conditional 0.322 [0.203, 0.452], passes → (b) is chosen** under the rule. Reference fixed-USDT A, k: 0.683. Caveats, reported rather than hidden: (b)'s CI upper end (0.45) is above the pass mark, so the pass is not robust; (b) over-predicts λ on 9 of 10 test days (mean bias +0.31, i.e. ~36% too many fills); its worst day is 2024-03-04 (0.78), whose σ is outside the calibration range. (a) fails because relative units fix the price-level drift but not the volatility drift: it under-predicts on volatile days (−0.96 on 2023-10-25, −1.29 on 2024-03-04) and over-predicts on quiet ones (+0.89 on 2023-11-12). - 2026-11-26 — Inventory is counted in lots inside the simulator and passed to strategies in lots; γ is risk aversion **per lot** and stays at 1e-4 (stage 2's main value). — The A–S formulas then give the same quotes per lot as in stage 2, so changing the lot changes only the fills (A, k) and the USDT scale. ### Step 2: adverse selection from the data
- 2026-11-26 — Markout = mid move against the passive side, −s·(m_{t+h} − m₀), s = +1 if the maker bought (is_buyer_maker), m₀ = the mid in force just before the trade, m_{t+h} = the first 1 s snapshot at or after t + h (so "h" means h to h + 1 s). Each aggTrade counts once (trade-weighted). Buckets of δ = |price − m₀|: touch, 0.1–0.2, 0.2–0.5, 0.5–1, 1–2, 2–3, 3–5, 5–10, ≥10 USDT. — A first pass with a single ≥5 bucket was too coarse where a markout-aware quote sits, so 2–5 and ≥5 were split before anything was fitted; the bucket edges were chosen on calibration days only.
- 2026-11-26 — Two trades (one each on 2024-01-28 and 2024-02-04) had no prior quote; they are dropped and counted, not filled.
- 2026-11-26 — OFI_N(s) uses trades in seconds s−N … s−1 only; the target is the next h s mid move in bps from the 1 s snapshots. The out-of-sample R² uses "no move" as the benchmark (1 − SSE/Σy²), and the hit rate is sign agreement on non-zero moves. — The martingale is the natural no-skill benchmark for price moves.
- 2026-11-26 — Main signal rule, fixed on calibration data before the test-day numbers were read: the (N, raw/imbalance) pair with the highest calibration R² averaged over h = 5 and 30 s. Result: the 5 s imbalance (B−S)/(B+S). Raw OFI over 30–60 s has a *negative* slope at h = 30 s (large flow partly reverts), and the imbalance is scale-free across days.
- 2026-11-26 — Simulator flow parameters, all from calibration days: z = imbalance / 0.624 (its calibration std); β(h) at h = 1…120 s fitted by κτ(1−e^(−h/τ)) → κ = 0.0453 bp/s per z, τ = 2.48 s (β peaks at ~20 s and then falls slightly; the OU curve cannot show that reversal); fill tilt θ = 0.257 at δ = 2 USDT (0.38 at 0.1, 0.26 at 3; δ = 2 is about the A–S half-spread). Check: the measured E[z | fill] is 0.15–0.26 by bucket, against θ = 0.26 in the simulator.
- 2026-11-26 — Markout jump J(δ) = the pooled calibration-day 60 s markout curve **in bps**, × the regime's S₀, minus the drift part κτ(1−e^(−60/τ))θ ≈ 0.08 USDT that the simulator's drift already produces after a fill (so that at predictive power ×1 the simulated 60 s markout matches the data). — In bps the curve is stable out of sample (0.96–1.16 bp calibration vs 0.98–1.16 test beyond the touch), while USDT markouts rise ~50% with the price. The per-regime USDT curves rest on 3 days each and have unstable tails (the low-regime ≥10 bucket was negative).
- 2026-11-26 — Jump noise sd = √(var of the 1 s markout around its bucket mean, δ ∈ [0.1, 10)) − 1.5 s·σ²), per regime: 3.0 / 6.5 / 10.5 USDT. — The size of the immediate jump varies; the 1 s markout less ~1.5 s of normal diffusion measures that spread. It affects only the P&L variance.
- 2026-11-26 — The fill tilt θ stays at its measured value across the predictive-power sweep; the sweep scales only κ (the drift, i.e. the predictive power the owner defined). J is fixed at its ×1 calibration, so at ×0 the simulated markout is 0.08 USDT below the data and at ×2 it is 0.08 above.
- 2026-11-26 — In informed mode the diffusion noise is σ_ε² = σ² − 2κ²τ, so that the long-run variance stays σ²_60s (the drift adds 2κ²τ). The drift's share of the variance is 6.5% / 2.4% / 0.9% (low / medium / high).

### Step 4: flow-aware A–S
- 2026-11-26 — Reservation shift D = κ̂τ_x(1 − e^(−(T−t)/τ_x))·x, the expected move of the mid until T from the current signal (μ̂·τ_eff). A constant drift would give μ(T−t), but the signal decays in ~2.5 s, so it is μ̂·τ_x. — Taken literally, μ̂·(T−t) with an hour left would multiply a 2-second prediction by 3,600.
- 2026-11-26 — "Widen the side about to be picked off": in this model the shift of r by D already widens the threatened side by D (measured from the mid) and tightens the other by D. No separate asymmetric term is added, because it would count the same expected move twice (derivation in src/mms/strategies/flow_aware.py).
- 2026-11-26 — Markout allowance: both half-spreads get + (δ*_rn − 1/k − cS), where δ*_rn = argmax e^(−kδ)(δ − cS − J(δ)). This equals J for a flat curve (the fee argument again) and is smaller than adding J(δ) at the quoting distance when J rises with δ (hand-worked test). — It is the exact risk-neutral answer for the measured curve, and in an interview it reduces to "price the markout like a fee".
- 2026-11-26 — The flow-aware strategy uses the calibrated κ̂ (×1) whatever the true predictive power in the sweep. — A live strategy does not know the true value; at ×0 the sweep shows what trusting a dead signal costs.
- 2026-11-26 — Post-only: the flow-aware quotes stay at least half a tick (0.05) from the mid. — A maker order cannot cross the book.
- 2026-11-26 — Added a fifth strategy as an ablation: "markout-aware A–S" = flow-aware with the signal ignored (D = 0). — Without it, "does flow-awareness recover the adverse selection?" would mix up pricing the average markout (no signal needed) with reading the flow.

### Step 5: comparison
- 2026-11-26 — Stage 3 seeds 20,000–20,999; γ = 1e-4 per lot; 0.01 BTC lot; T = 1 h, dt = 0.2 s; model (b) parameters per regime; fees −0.5 / 0 / 2 bp, plus a fee grid of −1 … 4 bp for break-even fees. Naive and A–S quotes do not depend on the fee, so they are simulated once and repriced exactly; the fee-dependent strategies are re-run (and re-optimised) at each fee.
- 2026-11-26 — **Bug found in the first full run and fixed before reporting:** with large rebates, quotes went to or through the mid. (i) At −2 bp, fee-aware A–S's half-spread was negative, so it quoted through the mid, filled almost every step and booked 98–166 USDT/h of "rebate", which an exchange would never pay (a crossing order is a taker). (ii) At −0.5 bp the markout-aware/flow-aware optimum was pulled to the touch, where the measured markout drops from 2.6 to 0.3 USDT. The A–S inventory term then put the quotes at ~0.3 USDT, on the steep part of J, so they lost money (and this showed up as a spurious "signal value" of +2.8 USDT/h even at predictive power ×0). Fix: in stage 3 runs every quote is kept **at least 0.1 USDT from the mid** (EnvConfig.min_distance; counted and reported), and the markout allowance is optimised over δ ≥ 0.1. — λ(δ) and J(δ) are calibrated only beyond the touch (δ ≥ 0.1): the touch needs a queue model, which we do not have (stage 2: excluding the touch = assuming we sit at the back of the queue). The fee grid now starts at −1 bp; −2 bp is beyond any real rebate (best ~−0.5 bp).
- 2026-11-26 — Break-even fee for fee-dependent strategies: only fees where the strategy is active (≥ 1 fill/h on average) count. — Above ~2 bp the markout-aware/flow-aware strategies stop quoting anything that fills, and their P&L ≈ 0 there produced a spurious "break-even at 3 bp" root.
- **Step 5 results (summary; full tables in results/stage3/answers.md, REPORT.md).** Adverse selection takes 87% [84, 89] of A–S's P&L at 0 bp (medium; 75–109% across regimes) and 48–60% at −0.5 bp. Markout-aware quoting recovers 11–26% of the fee-aware A–S adverse-selection cost at 0 bp; the order-flow signal adds nothing measurable (flow-aware − markout-aware within ±0.1 USDT/h at ×0–×2 predictive power). At −0.5 bp, plain A–S beats both, because its inventory skew turns many jumps in its favour (a jump after a fill revalues the whole remaining position, which the per-fill markout argument ignores). Break-even fees (informed): naive −0.50 / −0.37 / −0.01 bp, A–S −0.05 / +0.10 / +0.22 bp, fee-aware −0.62 / +0.65 / +0.96 bp; markout/flow-aware never lose but stop trading from +2 bp.

## Stage 4: replay backtester with a queue model

### Owner decisions on the stage 3 questions (2026-11-26)
- RL target: spread width and skew, not trading on the signal; RL moves to stage 5, after this replay environment exists. The OFI signal stays available as an observation.
- Build a queue model now from the real data; stage 5 trains on the calibration days and tests on the test days. Reward: per-step mark-to-market P&L minus an inventory penalty matched to γ.

### Step 0: data
- 2026-11-26 — data/processed/ held only 1 s snapshots (no 100 ms sizes, no within-bucket min/max), so each of the 20 days was re-downloaded (bookTicker + aggTrades, SHA-256 verified), reduced and its raw zips deleted (scripts/build_stage4_dataset.py, resumable; outputs in data/processed/stage4/). The stage 2 1 s files are kept for the earlier stages.
- 2026-11-26 — 100 ms row = state at the *end* of the bucket (last update; carried forward when the bucket has no update, since the book did not change; NaN before the day's first update). min/max size are over the observations of the bucket's *final* price only (the carried-in state if at that price, plus every update at that price). — A size at another price is a different queue; the min is used as a hard cap on the queue ahead.
- 2026-11-26 — Trades: every aggTrade with ms time, price, qty, aggressor side and the best bid/ask and sizes in force strictly before it (the stage 2 rule), plus its 100 ms bucket.

### Step 1: queue model and backtester (src/mms/replay/)
- 2026-11-26 — Prices are integer ticks inside the replay. — "Same price" must be an exact comparison.
- 2026-11-26 — Order of events inside a 100 ms bucket is unknown; the replay applies (1) trade-through, (2) traded volume at our price against the queue, (3) book crossing, (4) unexplained size decrease C = max(0, size_prev − size_min − V) split by the cancel rule, (5) cap queue ahead ≤ min displayed size. — Trades first is conservative; the cap is a physical constraint (orders ahead only leave, and the displayed size = orders ahead + behind, our order being virtual), so it applies under every rule, including pessimistic.
- 2026-11-26 — Proportional rule: cancels ahead = C × Q / (size_prev − V). Optimistic: min(C, Q). Pessimistic: 0 (but the size cap still applies).
- 2026-11-26 — Joining a level *behind* the touch: its size is not in the best-bid/ask feed, so the queue ahead is unknown (inf, "back" convention): prints at our price do not fill us, only a trade *through* our price or the level becoming best (queue = its displayed size then). An upper-bound "front" convention (queue 0) is run for the step 2 validation. — The pessimistic choice for unseen depth; bracketed by "front".
- 2026-11-26 — A level that stops being best keeps our queue position unchanged while unobserved (we never cancelled), and is capped by the displayed size when it is best again. If our price ends up *better* than the real best (everyone at it cancelled), queue ahead = 0: we would be alone there.
- 2026-11-26 — Book crossing our quote (best ask ≤ our bid) = fill in full. — The real book never crosses, so the level was empty and the incoming order would have matched us.
- 2026-11-26 — Partial fills are allowed (fill = volume beyond the queue, up to our remaining quantity); the rest stays at the front of the queue. — aggTrades go down to 0.001 BTC; rounding to whole lots would be arbitrary.
- 2026-11-26 — Latency applies to both new orders and cancels (a request at t takes effect at t + latency); post-only: a bid at or above the best ask at activation is rejected and counted. — Symmetric and realistic for an exchange gateway.
- 2026-11-26 — Inventory limit ±10 lots: an order is sized so that a full fill cannot breach the limit (only one order per side, so it cannot be breached while resting); at the limit that side is rejected and counted.
- 2026-11-26 — Strategies see the recorded book, which does not contain our own orders. — Limitation of a replay; noted in the report.
- 2026-11-26 — Features at decision time t use only data before t: 5 s OFI imbalance / 0.624 (stage 3 signal), stage 3 trailing σ̂ (EWMA span 30 min of 60 s changes), typical size = trailing 30-minute median of (bid size + ask size)/2 sampled each second.
- 2026-11-26 — Episodes: 23 hour-long episodes per day (01:00–24:00 UTC; 00:00–01:00 is the warm-up of σ̂ and typical size), so 230 per split. CIs bootstrap episodes, as asked; a day-block bootstrap is reported alongside for the main table, because hours within a day are correlated.
- 2026-11-26 — Markouts of our fills: −s(m_{t+h} − m₀), m₀ = the mid at the decision time of the step in which the fill happened; fills in the first 59 minutes of an episode only (so t + 60 s stays inside the hour, as in the synthetic comparison).

### Step 3: thresholds (rule fixed before any test-day run)
- 2026-11-26 — Each strategy's threshold is chosen on the 10 calibration days only (230 episodes), proportional cancel rule, 100 ms latency, 0.01 BTC lot, as the grid value with the highest mean P&L per hour, **separately for each fee scenario** (−0.5, 0, +2 bp). Grids: skew max_lots ∈ {1, 2, 5, 10} × γ_skew ∈ {0, 1e-5, 1e-4}; queue-aware θ ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2} × typical size; OFI-aware z* ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.25, 1.5} (|z| ≤ 1.6 by construction). — A trader knows the fee tier in advance, and fills do not depend on the fee, so per-fee tuning costs nothing and is not a look at test data.
- 2026-11-26 — Touch + skew: the A–S reservation shift q·γ·σ̂²·(T − t) rounded to ticks moves the inventory-growing side behind the touch and lets the reducing side improve inside the spread (never crossing); the growing side is pulled at |q| ≥ max_lots.
- 2026-11-26 — Markout-aware A–S in the replay: stage 3's class with σ = σ̂ and k = model (b) at σ̂ at the start of each episode, J = the stage 3 medium-regime markout curve in bps of the mid × the current mid (δ grid in USDT as in stage 3), quotes ≥ 0.1 USDT from the mid, rounded outward to the tick.
- 2026-11-26 — Seeds: step 2 synthetic predictions use seeds 40,000–40,199 for every hour. The stage 3 RL seed-range proposal is not recorded in the repo, so for stage 5's synthetic sanity checks: train 100,000–199,999, test 200,000–200,999 (disjoint from stages 1–4: 0–999, 10,000–10,999, 20,000–20,999, 40,000–40,199). The replay RL environment uses calibration days for training and test days for evaluation; a seed picks a day and a start uniformly on the 100 ms grid between 01:00 and 23:00.
- **Tuning result (calibration days, 2026-11-26).** Every touch configuration loses at every fee (always-at-touch −35.1 [−38.9, −31.4] USDT/h at 0 bp, −9.9 at −0.5 bp). The chosen thresholds are all at the low-activity edge of their grids (skew max_lots = 1, queue θ = 0.05, OFI z* = 0.25): on these days quoting less always loses less, so the calibration data itself says the best touch policy in this grid is to trade as little as possible. The grids were fixed before running and are not extended after seeing this (extending towards zero activity would only converge on "don't quote"). Chosen: skew (γ_skew 0, max 1 lot) at −0.5 and 0 bp, (γ_skew 1e-4, max 1 lot) at +2 bp; queue θ = 0.05; OFI z* = 0.25 at every fee. Full grid: results/stage4/tuning.md.

### Run notes
- 2026-11-26 — The first test-day run was stopped by Claude Code because the machine ran low on memory (14 workers × ~1.7 GB, plus the download). On the owner's instruction it was restarted with 6 workers (each worker caches at most 3 days, so ~1.7 GB is a ceiling) and finished. - 2026-11-26 — Synthetic predictions process each hour's 200 paths in chunks of at most 3.6 million path-steps. — The crash hour on 2024-03-04 has σ̂ = 34.8, A = 10.3/s, so A·dt < 0.1 needs dt = 0.00625 s (576,000 steps): unchunked it would need ~10–14 GB. Results are identical with and without chunking (tested), since each path's randomness comes from its own seed.
- 2026-11-26 — Synthetic dt per hour: 0.2 s, halved until A·dt < 0.1 (187 hours at 0.2 s, 26 at 0.1, 11 at 0.05, 5 at 0.025, 1 at 0.00625). Jump noise sd interpolated linearly in σ between the three stage 3 regimes (flat beyond them); it only affects variance.
- 2026-11-26 — Owner decision: the pre-registered grids are the main result and are not widened; 0 ms latency and the optimistic cancel rule are kept as best-case bounds.

### Step 3 results (test days, 230 paired hours; tables in results/stage4/)
- No touch configuration makes money in any scenario: 4 strategies × 3 cancel rules × 3 latencies × 3 fees, every mean negative and every 95% CI upper bound below 0. Best: queue-aware at 300 ms and −0.5 bp, −5.6 [−7.7, −3.7] USDT/h. At 100 ms, 0 bp, proportional: always-at-touch −74.4 [−84.6, −64.7], skew −45.6, queue-aware −20.8, OFI-aware −39.4, markout-aware A–S −1.34 [−1.76, −0.89].
- Per BTC traded every touch variant loses about the same, ~0.80–0.93 bp at 0 bp: the "improvements" are almost entirely from trading less, not from better fills.
- Our touch fills: 58% (proportional) come from sweeps through our price, 60 s markout 4.94 USDT/BTC (1.09 bp); fills from the queue 1.46 (0.35 bp); the half-spread captured is ~0.05–0.14. The −0.5 bp rebate (~2 USDT/BTC at ~40k) would make queue fills alone profitable, but not the sweeps.
- Lower latency makes things *worse* in USDT/h (more fills, each losing); the cancel rule changes fill volume (optimistic +24% vs proportional for always-at-touch) but barely changes P&L per BTC.
- Touch strategies other than skew hit the ±10-lot inventory limit in every episode; their results include a one-sided inventory position.
